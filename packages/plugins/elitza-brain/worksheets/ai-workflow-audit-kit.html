Before you start

Broken workflow automation fails quietly. The form fires, the CRM receives the contact, sales sees the name. Everything looks alive. Then the contact has no source, the deal never opens, and the owner field stays blank. Nobody notices until the follow-up window has closed.

The failure is rarely in the model. It is in the wiring, the retries and the missing audit trail. This kit finds the wiring.

Ground rules: audit the money path first. Lead intake, lead scoring, lead routing, deal creation, follow-up, payment handoff, onboarding, reporting. Everything else waits. Take the inventory before you change anything. Write down what you find while it is still true.

Section 3 · Failure signals that dashboards never show

Twelve patterns I find over and over. Tick what you can prove is happening, then carry the ticks into Section 4. Each line tells you where to look: the signal is rarely where the pain shows up.

1. Silent webhook failures: the event never arrives and nothing pages anyone. Where to look: endpoint logs, provider delivery history, any step with no failure handler.
2. Duplicate writes: the same contact exists three times with different owners, and every report downstream is wrong. Where to look: creation logic with no search-before-create, form resubmits, retry storms.
3. Missing idempotency: a replayed event creates a second record instead of updating the first. Where to look: no idempotency_key, no event_id stored, no raw payload archive.
4. Stale state: lifecycle stage sits in New for three days while the deal never opens. Where to look: stage transitions with no logger, filters that stop a path silently.
5. No audit trail: nobody can say what ran, what changed, or who approved it. Where to look: missing correlation_id, no before and after values, run history past retention.
6. Permission drift: a token expired, a scope narrowed, or an API key was rotated while the workflow kept calling. Where to look: 401 and 403 responses buried in module logs, service accounts with no owner.
7. Blank owner assignment: the record exists, the owner field stays empty, nothing routes. Where to look: routing rules keyed on a field the form never sends.
8. Dead owner IDs: a user left, the CRM kept the ID alive, leads land in an inbox nobody reads. Where to look: owner status checks in the routing layer.
9. Field map drift: a property was renamed six months ago and the chain still writes the old name, the old pipeline ID, the old trigger condition. Where to look: any scenario built months ago with no audit since.
10. Retries that swallow the error: the chain stops at step four, retries twice, then gives up quietly. Where to look: ignore-error flags, modules with no error handler, filters doing the job of error handling.
11. Filters as lead graveyards: one missing field blocks a good record and no queue ever holds it. Where to look: every filter sitting between a lead and the CRM.
12. Unowned automation chains: nobody has audited or tested them, and they run every day. Where to look: anything with no owner, no purpose, no last-tested date.

Triage rule: a quick fix is a config change, a field map correction, an owner ID update or an error handler you forgot to attach. A rebuild is anything that needs a new contract between systems: identity rules, idempotency, a decision object, a schema, or a migration. Do not paper a rebuild with a filter.

The five-step procedure I run on a scoped rebuild

Every intake runs the same priority order. The order is the diagnosis: fix the layer that silently breaks everything above it, before touching anything visible.

STEP 1The trigger and the payload

What I check: does the event fire, with the fields the next step actually expects?

Evidence to collect: one raw payload per source, the provider delivery log, the schema you believe is correct.

Pass condition: required fields present, types correct, event_id and correlation_id on every message, consent captured where it matters.

Common failure: a form label changed, the payload shape drifted, one required field came through empty and the chain ran anyway.

STEP 2The identity layer

What I check: dedupe rules, matching keys and owner assignment before anything writes.

Evidence to collect: search results for the last 20 records, the idempotency key format, the owner resolution rule.

Pass condition: search contact by email and company by domain before create, one idempotency key per source event, owner resolves to an active user every time.

Common failure: creation with no search, inactive owner IDs still sitting in the routing rule.

STEP 3The write path

What I check: schemas, permissions and idempotency on every CRM update.

Evidence to collect: the field map, token scopes and expiry, rate limit behaviour, raw API response codes.

Pass condition: every write validates against a schema, every retry is safe to replay, every permission is scoped and owned.

Common failure: renamed property, expired token, a retry that creates a second record instead of updating.

STEP 4The failure path

What I check: retries, dead-letter handling and alerting, so the next break announces itself instead of hiding.

Evidence to collect: an error handler on every module, the contents of any dead-letter queue, where alerts are routed.

Pass condition: every failure lands somewhere visible, with its payload, its correlation ID and a named owner.

Common failure: ignore-error flags, filters used as error handling, alerts sent to a channel nobody watches.

STEP 5The audit trail

What I check: a log of what ran, what changed and who approved it.

Evidence to collect: correlation ID threaded end to end, before and after values on each write, approval records on high-value changes.

Pass condition: any record can be reconstructed from its event_id, including the raw payload and the rule decision that produced it.

Common failure: run history past retention, no raw payload archive, no approval record on anything that touched money or a client.

Evidence pack for the stakeholder review

Three things make an audit fast. Two of them are optional, none of them are difficult. Assemble the pack before you open a single scenario. Half the value of an audit is not re-reading the same log three times.

  • Required: what breaks, which systems are involved, and what the expected behaviour was.
  • Helpful: error logs, webhook payloads, schema samples. Speed it up, not mandatory.
  • Helpful: the spreadsheet someone keeps because they stopped trusting the CRM. It is a confession and a map at the same time.

Section 6 · FAQ

Do I need anyone on the call to run this?

No. You submit the stack and the symptoms, or you work the worksheets yourself. A conversation only happens when you ask for one.

Which workflows do I audit first?

The ones touching money. Lead capture, lead scoring, lead routing, deal creation, follow-up, payment handoff, onboarding, reporting. Everything else waits.

What stacks does it cover?

Make.com, Zapier, n8n, HubSpot, Salesforce, GoHighLevel, Clio, MyCase, Airtable, webhooks and custom APIs. If it moves data between two systems, it is in scope.

What do I walk away with?

A completed inventory, a ticked failure checklist, a ranked fix list in four tiers with the reasoning behind each ranking, and a triage lane for each fix: quick fix or full rebuild.

Can you implement the fixes too?

Yes. The diagnosis stands alone. Implementation runs through the AI workflow automation engagement. When the break is the data layer itself, the CRM Automation Repair Kit is the matching fix sheet.

What if the break sits between two business systems?

That usually belongs to a CRM and API workflow overhaul rather than a single workflow. The kit still maps it, and the ranking tells you where the boundary is.

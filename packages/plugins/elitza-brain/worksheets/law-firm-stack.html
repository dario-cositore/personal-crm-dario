Part 1 · Automate Lead Intake in MyCase, From Form Fill to Matter

The same four stage intake I built for D'Alessio Law Group, written out step by step. You get capture, validation, conflict checks, matter creation behind an approval gate, and the SLA timer that escalates.

Paralegals reading emails, opening MyCase, creating contact and matter records, assigning attorneys, triggering follow-ups. Every one of those keystrokes is latency. This kit replaces the keystrokes and keeps the judgment where it belongs: with a human who signs. On the D'Alessio Law Group build that took 22 hours a week of paralegal time off the intake desk.

The rule that governs everything below: the system may prepare anything, it may never file or send without approval. Every step below says which side of that line it sits on.

Proof this works in production. D'Alessio Law Group ran high-cost campaigns with intake on email and manual entry. I rebuilt the path as four stages: inbound capture, qualification, attorney routing and dual-CRM sync into Lawmatics and MyCase. Response time moved from 6 to 48 hours to about 10 seconds, and 22 hours a week of paralegal time went back to client work. Read the full D'Alessio Law Group case study for the before and after.

Section 1 · Capture: store the raw payload before you parse it

Four layers run in order: capture and spam filtering, document extraction when a file is attached, native MyCase injection, then the notification cascade. Work top to bottom. Each step names its system, its gate and what happens when it fails.

S1Capture the submission

System: Typeform, Gravity Forms or your website form, webhooks into Make.com or n8n.

Input: raw submission including case details, contact data, consent flag, and any uploaded document reference.

Gate: none yet. Store the raw payload before you parse it.

Failure: endpoint returns non-2xx so the provider retries, payload persisted for replay.

S2Spam and sanity filter

System: validation module in your orchestrator.

Gate: IP reputation, velocity check, honeypot field, email format, required fields present.

Output: pass or reject with the reason attached. Rejected items go to a review queue, never to MyCase.

Failure: reject logged with reason. A public webhook with no signature check will get hit by test submissions, so verify the HMAC before this step.

S3Extract the document, when one is attached

System: Anthropic API call through your deterministic wrapper, JSON schema enforced.

Gate: schema validation. Non-JSON output is rejected and retried with a simplified prompt.

Output: structured fields only: case type, damages, jurisdiction, urgency, parties, dates, obligations.

Failure: two failed extractions route the document to human review. Never guess a number.

S4Field mapping into MyCase vocabulary

System: translation table between intake fields and MyCase case types and custom fields.

Gate: every mapped value exists in the target vocabulary. Unmapped values land in a fallback store, not silently dropped.

Example: case_type becomes matter_type_id.

Failure: unmapped field goes to the fallback table with a daily alert. On the D'Alessio build, custom fields did not map one to one into MyCase and data fell through the gap until we added a field translation dictionary with fallback logic.

Section 2 · Conflict and duplicate checks that stop the pipeline

Before anything is created, the pipeline looks for the person and for an ethical conflict. A conflict stops the pipeline and escalates. It does not create a record and it does not assume clean on a timeout.

S5Duplicate and conflict check

System: MyCase contact search by email and phone, party lookup against the matters database.

Gate: if a conflict is detected, stop the pipeline and escalate. Do not create anything.

Output: existing_contact, clean, or conflict_detected with the matching party IDs.

Failure: lookup timeout routes to a manual queue with a timer. Never assume clean on a timeout.

Section 3 · Matter creation behind a human approval gate

Matter creation is the step that opens a file. It does not run unattended on first contact. The system stages everything, writes an approval request, and waits for a named human.

Approval queue rules

One approver role per action type. Nobody approves their own automated step.
Queue has an SLA. Unactioned items escalate, they do not silently age.
Rejection is a valid outcome and routes the item to a named human, not back into the loop.
Every approval and rejection is immutable. Corrections append, they do not overwrite.
Bulk approval is disabled. Approving ten matters at once is not approval.
Idempotency key on every write. Replay a submission twice and the second call must return the stored result.

Section 4 · Notification cascade and the SLA timer

Once a matter is approved and created, people need to know. The cascade fires in a fixed order, consent is checked before every client-facing channel, and the SLA timer starts with the matter so a scenario restart does not reset the clock.

Notification cascade

N1Attorney alert

Channel: Slack or Teams to the assigned attorney.

Content: case type, urgency and jurisdiction in the message body, not behind a link.

Failure: a notification failure pages the on-call path. It does not roll back the matter.

N2Client SMS where opt-in exists

Channel: Twilio or equivalent, only when consent_to_contact is true.

Content: acknowledgement only. No legal advice, no fee discussion, no case assessment.

Failure: logged and retried once, then email path takes over.

N3Email sequence and calendar

Channel: firm email sequence, calendar invite with prep documents attached.

Content: intake confirmation, what to prepare, who will call.

Failure: sequence pause + ops alert. Do not double-send after a partial failure.

N4Firm channel summary

Channel: internal digest, batched, not per-lead spam.

Content: counts, conflicts flagged, matters opened, anything in the review queue.

Failure: the digest is optional. The attorney alert is not.

Section 5 · What broke in production, and the fix for each

Failures I hit building this path in production, including on the D'Alessio Law Group intake. Each one has a fix you should start with rather than discover yourself.

01MyCase API field mismatch

Broke: custom intake fields did not map one to one to MyCase fields, and data fell through the gap.

Fix: field translation dictionary with fallback logic. Unmapped values store in a fallback table with a daily report, never silently dropped.

02Webhook flood on campaign launch

Broke: a major campaign triggered 847 leads in 4 hours and the rate limits threw errors across the pipeline.

Fix: buffer layer with exponential backoff and queue-based rate limiting in front of every CRM call. Confirm the request ceiling you are allowed and enforce it in code.

03Schema strictness on extraction

Broke: when document quality dropped, the model returned free text and the JSON parse step failed downstream.

Fix: a response wrapper, a validation step that rejects non-JSON, and one retry with a simplified extraction prompt before escalation.

04Webhook security

Broke: public webhooks were triggered by test submissions, creating junk matters.

Fix: HMAC signature verification at the edge before any webhook is processed.

05Retry doubling the file

Broke: a retry after a timeout created a second matter for the same submission.

Fix: idempotency key per submission on every create. Replay returns the stored result instead of writing again.

Pre-cutover checklist

Field translation table complete, including the fallback path for unmapped values.
Token bucket and backoff in front of the MyCase API. Confirm the rate limit you are allowed.
Idempotency key on every write, tested by replaying one submission twice.
Approval gate proven: attempt an unattended matter creation and watch it stop.
Conflict check returning hits on a known adverse party fixture.
Dead-letter queue populated by an intentional failure, with a named owner.
Consent flags checked before any client-facing channel fires.
SLA timer survives a scenario restart.
One person trained to read the audit trail without asking me first.

Section 6 · FAQ

Does this only work with MyCase?

The SOP and the gates are platform independent. This kit writes the MyCase field names and API calls explicitly. If you run Clio Manage and HubSpot instead, the Clio Manage to HubSpot Sync Map is the matching integration layer.

Where do attached documents go for extraction?

Directly to the model API through your own wrapper with a zero-data-retention configuration. Not through a general-purpose SaaS middleman. The Contract OCR API Pack has the prompt, schemas and validation layer for that step.

What still needs a person every time?

Matter creation, anything sent to a client, anything filed, anything touching money, and any conflict result. See Section 3.

What if extraction is wrong?

Escalation criteria catch it before the record is written: low confidence, missing required fields, numeric conflict, ambiguous jurisdiction, two failed attempts. The reviewer corrects it, and the correction is stored beside the original.

How long does a build like this take?

The D'Alessio Law Group intake ran as a four stage system covering capture, qualification, attorney routing and dual-CRM sync. The SOP here is the intake half: form fill to case record with no manual entry. Your timeline depends on how many intake channels you run and how clean your MyCase vocabulary already is.

Can you build it for me instead?

Yes. The kit stands alone, and the same pipeline ships as a client engagement through document processing agents and the wider workflow automation services.

What happens next. Run the free workflow diagnostic on your current intake first, or read the D'Alessio Law Group case study, then decide. If your firm also keeps contacts in HubSpot, the Clio Manage to HubSpot Sync Map covers the round trip between intake and sales.

Part 2 · Contract OCR API: Clean JSON Out of Messy PDFs

Template based OCR breaks the moment a field moves an inch. You get the vision prompt, the enforced schemas and the validation layer I use to turn contracts and filings into strict JSON.

This pack is the extraction layer I put in front of any document pipeline that has to produce structured records instead of a pile of text. The vision model reads the page in context. The schema decides what is allowed to leave. The validator decides what is allowed to write. You get the prompt, the schemas, the thresholds, the retry wording and the Python call that runs all of it.

Before you start

Template based OCR treats a page as a fixed coordinate map. Field at x, y. Label at x2, y2. It works on one clean scan and falls apart on everything else: a retainer with a new letterhead, a lease with an addendum stapled on, a court filing where the caption moved down a line, an ID photo taken at an angle. A vision-language model reads the page the way a person does. Layout, tables, handwriting, shifted blocks. The schema is what keeps that read honest.

Ground rules: never let the model write directly into a system of record. Extraction produces a candidate payload. Validation decides if the payload is safe. Confidence decides if a human sees it. Only then does anything write. Keep the original document beside every extracted object so any field can be traced back to a page.

Section 2 · The extraction prompt and its output rules

One prompt template, parameterised by document type and target schema. The rules are hard on purpose. A prompt that allows prose allows silent parse failures downstream.

Extraction prompt template

ROLE
You extract structured fields from legal documents. You never
summarize, never infer, never invent.

INPUT
document_type: {{engagement | nda | service_agreement | lease |
                court_filing | identity | contract}}
matter_hint: {{case_type if known}}
target_schema: {{schema_name}}
page_count_hint: {{expected pages, or null}}

RULES
1. Return JSON only. No prose before or after.
2. Wrap output exactly as:
   <response><json>{...}</json></response>
3. Read numbers as they appear in the document. Spell-out
   numbers convert to digits: "thirty-six thousand six hundred
   five" becomes 36605. If the digits and the words disagree,
   set numeric_conflict: true and use neither value.
4. Dates become YYYY-MM-DD. If only a partial date exists,
   set date_precision to year, month or day.
5. Jurisdiction is the stated one. If absent or ambiguous,
   return null. Do not guess from the party names.
6. Every field in the required list must be present. Use null
   for genuinely absent values, never an empty string.
7. Quote source text for each extracted value under
   "evidence". If you cannot locate the source text, the
   field is null.
8. Party fields capture the full legal name as written.
   Roles are separate fields. Do not merge a company and its
   signatory into one string.
9. Currency is ISO 4217. If the document states a symbol with
   no currency code, infer the code only when the symbol is
   unambiguous for the stated jurisdiction. Otherwise
   currency: null and amount: null.
10. Never average, round or normalise a figure the document
    states. Transcribe, then let code normalise.
11. If two sections disagree on the same fact, keep both under
    "conflicts" and set the primary field to null.
12. If the document is unreadable, return
    {"status":"unreadable","reason":"..."}.

OUTPUT
JSON object conforming to the target schema.

What each rule prevents

RuleFailure it stopsWhere it bites if you drop it
1 JSON onlyMarkdown fences and commentary around the objectEvery downstream parse step
2 response / json wrapperModel talking past a strict extractorRetry logic, logging, schema registry
3 digit vs word numbersInvented numeric conversionsFees, deposits, penalties, damages
4 date precisionFalse day-level confidence on a month-only dateNotice windows, limitation periods
5 jurisdiction as statedGuessed governing law from a party addressVenue, conflict checks, court routing
6 null not empty stringEmpty writes that look like real valuesCRM required fields, search indexes
7 evidence per fieldFields nobody can trace back to a pageReviewers, audits, corrections
8 party roles separateCompany and signatory collapsed into one nameDedupe, conflict check, service lists
9 currency explicitSilent USD assumption on a EUR documentEvery money field
10 transcribe firstModel rounding 36605 into 36600Fee schedules, escalators
11 conflicts arrayOne of two disagreeing values chosen at randomAmendments, riders, prior drafts
12 unreadable statusConfident junk from a blank or corrupt pageBatch jobs that would write garbage

Retry wording (use this, not a longer prompt)

When the first pass fails envelope parse or schema validation, retry once with a reduced prompt. Do not argue with the model. Narrow the job.

RETRY
The previous response did not validate against the target
schema. Return JSON only, wrapped as
<response><json>{...}</json></response>.

Extract ONLY these fields:
{{comma separated required field list}}

If a field is not on the page, return null.
If two sections disagree, set the field to null and list the
quotations under "conflicts".
Do not explain. Do not add fields.

Two attempts is the ceiling. A third attempt is superstition. After two failures the document goes to the reviewer queue with both raw responses attached.

Section 3 · Schemas that reject anything the model invents

One base envelope, six type-specific property sets. The envelope carries status, confidence, evidence and conflict state. The property sets carry the fields that actually differ by document. Validate the envelope first, then the property set for the type you requested. Anything not in the schema is dropped before it reaches a record.

Base envelope schema

{
  "$id": "ocr/envelope.v1.json",
  "type": "object",
  "properties": {
    "document_type": {"enum": ["engagement","nda","service_agreement",
                               "lease","court_filing","identity","contract"]},
    "target_schema": {"type": "string"},
    "schema_version": {"type": "string"},
    "page_count": {"type": "integer", "minimum": 1},
    "status": {"enum": ["ok", "partial", "unreadable", "conflict"]},
    "confidence": {"type": "number", "minimum": 0, "maximum": 1},
    "meets_threshold": {"type": "boolean"},
    "numeric_conflict": {"type": "boolean"},
    "date_precision": {"enum": ["day", "month", "year", null]},
    "jurisdiction": {"type": ["string", "null"]},
    "evidence": {
      "type": "object",
      "additionalProperties": {
        "type": "object",
        "properties": {
          "quote": {"type": "string"},
          "page": {"type": "integer", "minimum": 1}},
        "required": ["quote", "page"]}
    },
    "conflicts": {
      "type": "array",
      "items": {
        "type": "object",
        "properties": {
          "field": {"type": "string"},
          "values": {"type": "array", "items": {"type": "string"}},
          "pages": {"type": "array", "items": {"type": "integer"}}},
        "required": ["field", "values"]}
    },
    "fields": {"type": "object"}
  },
  "required": ["document_type","target_schema","schema_version","status",
               "confidence","meets_threshold","numeric_conflict",
               "evidence","fields"],
  "additionalProperties": false
}

Three points. additionalProperties: false is the rejection valve for invented keys. meets_threshold lets your orchestrator branch on a boolean instead of parsing free text. evidence is keyed by field name so a reviewer opens one object and sees both the claim and the page.

Section 4 · Validation: numbers, dates, cross references

Validation runs in code, not in the prompt. The prompt asks for honesty. The validator enforces it. Run every check below on every payload before anything writes.

Validation rules

Envelope check: the <response><json> wrapper parses, then the inner object parses as JSON. Reject and retry with the reduced prompt if not.
Type check: every field matches its declared type. Integer fields reject floats. Number fields reject strings with currency symbols still attached.
Required check: every field in the type's required list is present. Null is allowed only where the schema allows it. A required field that is null forces escalation.
Range check: amounts non-negative, notice days between 0 and your ceiling, confidence between 0 and 1, expiry dates in the past on identity documents.
Enum check: fee type, lease type, filing type, urgency and jurisdiction limited to values your CRM actually accepts. Anything else is a mapping failure, not a new value.
Numeric consistency: if digits and words disagree, or the same figure appears twice with different values, set numeric_conflict and stop. Language models hallucinate numeric conversions. This is the most expensive class of error in a legal document.
Date shape and order: values match their date_precision. End date after start date. Expiry after issue. Hearing date not before filing date. Notice windows consistent with the termination clause.
Cross-reference parties: party names appear in the evidence map. Each party name is checked against your conflict list and existing contact records before any write.
Evidence present: each required field has a source quote and a page. A field with no evidence is treated as absent.
Unknown key check: any property outside the schema is dropped and logged. The model does not get to invent fields.

Python: vision call, schema validation, routing

One module. Call the model with the document and the prompt. Parse the envelope. Validate against the base schema and the type property set. Run the cross-checks. Route on the result. This is the shape I ship.

import base64, json, re
import anthropic
from jsonschema import Draft7Validator

client = anthropic.Anthropic()
ENVELOPE = json.load(open("schemas/ocr/envelope.v1.json"))
SETS = json.load(open("schemas/ocr/property_sets.v1.json"))
FLOORS = {"engagement": 0.88, "nda": 0.86, "service_agreement": 0.86,
          "lease": 0.87, "court_filing": 0.85, "identity": 0.92,
          "contract": 0.85}
WRAPPER = re.compile(r"<response><json>(.*)</json></response>", re.S)
JURISDICTION_REQUIRED = {"engagement", "nda", "service_agreement",
                         "lease", "court_filing", "contract"}


def doc_block(path):
    data = open(path, "rb").read()
    b64 = base64.b64encode(data).decode()
    if path.endswith(".pdf"):
        return {"type": "document",
                "source": {"type": "base64",
                           "media_type": "application/pdf", "data": b64}}
    return {"type": "image",
            "source": {"type": "base64", "media_type": "image/png",
                       "data": b64}}


def parse_envelope(text):
    m = WRAPPER.search(text or "")
    if not m:
        raise ValueError("envelope_missing")
    return json.loads(m.group(1))


def validate_payload(payload, doc_type):
    errs = [f"envelope:{e.message}"
            for e in Draft7Validator(ENVELOPE).iter_errors(payload)]
    fields = payload.get("fields") or {}
    props = SETS[doc_type]["fields"]
    wrapped = {"type": "object", "properties": props,
               "additionalProperties": False}
    errs += [f"fields:{e.message}"
             for e in Draft7Validator(wrapped).iter_errors(fields)]
    for name in SETS[doc_type]["required_fields"]:
        if name not in fields:
            errs.append(f"missing_required:{name}")
        elif fields.get(name) is None and name != "jurisdiction":
            errs.append(f"null_required:{name}")
    return errs


def cross_checks(payload):
    errs = []
    fields = payload.get("fields") or {}
    if payload.get("numeric_conflict"):
        errs.append("numeric_conflict")
    pairs = [("effective_date", "end_date"), ("effective_date", "expiration_date"),
             ("start_date", "end_date"), ("commencement_date", "expiration_date"),
             ("issue_date", "expiry_date"), ("filing_date", "hearing_date")]
    for a, b in pairs:
        if fields.get(a) and fields.get(b) and str(fields[b]) <= str(fields[a]):
            errs.append(f"date_order:{a}>={b}")
    for name, cell in (payload.get("evidence") or {}).items():
        if not cell.get("quote") or not cell.get("page"):
            errs.append(f"evidence_incomplete:{name}")
    dt = payload.get("document_type")
    if dt in JURISDICTION_REQUIRED and not payload.get("jurisdiction"):
        errs.append("jurisdiction_missing")
    return errs


def retry_prompt(doc_type):
    required = ", ".join(SETS[doc_type]["required_fields"])
    return ("RETRY\nReturn JSON only, wrapped as "
            "<response><json>{...}</json></response>.\n"
            f"Extract ONLY these fields: {required}\n"
            "If a field is not on the page, return null. "
            "Do not explain. Do not add fields.")


def extract(path, doc_type, schema_name):
    system = "You extract structured fields from legal documents."
    base_prompt = open("prompts/extraction.v1.txt").read().format(
        document_type=doc_type, matter_hint="null",
        target_schema=schema_name, page_count_hint="null")
    prompt, raw = base_prompt, ""
    for attempt in (1, 2):
        msg = client.messages.create(
            model="claude-sonnet-4-5", max_tokens=4096, system=system,
            messages=[{"role": "user", "content": [
                doc_block(path), {"type": "text", "text": prompt}]}])
        raw = msg.content[0].text
        try:
            payload = parse_envelope(raw)
        except (ValueError, json.JSONDecodeError):
            prompt = retry_prompt(doc_type)
            continue
        errs = validate_payload(payload, doc_type) + cross_checks(payload)
        floor = FLOORS[doc_type]
        ok = (payload.get("confidence", 0) >= floor
              and not errs and not payload.get("numeric_conflict"))
        payload["meets_threshold"] = ok
        if ok:
            return {"result": "accepted", "payload": payload,
                    "attempt": attempt}
        prompt = retry_prompt(doc_type)
    reason = "two_failed_attempts" if errs else "below_confidence_floor"
    return {"result": "escalate", "payload": payload, "errors": errs,
            "raw_responses": [raw], "reason": reason}
Do not skip the validator because the model looked right. A payload can be fluent, complete and wrong on the fee. The validator is the only layer that does not care how convincing the JSON is.

Reviewer checklist

Open the source page beside the candidate payload. Not the summary. The page.
Confirm every required field against the evidence quote before changing anything.
Correct by writing a corrected object. Never overwrite the model output. Both versions stay.
Record why the model was wrong: layout, illegible, ambiguous clause, bad page split.
If the same reason appears three times in a week, change the prompt or the schema. Do not keep correcting by hand.
Close the item with a decision: accept as corrected, reject and re-request, or block for attorney sign-off.
Queue rules: one role per document type. SLA on every open item, escalating when it ages. Rejection is a valid outcome and routes to a named person, not back into the loop. Corrections append. Bulk close is disabled.

Section 6 · FAQ

Does this replace my current OCR tool?

It replaces the extraction and validation layer. If a tool already stores and versions documents, keep it. Point the tool at this call instead of its built-in template mapper.

Which model does the Python call use?

A vision-capable model API with zero data retention configured on your account. The sample uses an Anthropic messages call with a document block. Swap the client, keep the schema and the validator.

Why not just ask the model harder?

Because a prompt cannot enforce a type. It can only request one. The schema rejects invented keys, the validator rejects bad values, and the queue catches the rest. Three layers, each one mechanical.

What about handwritten signatures and stamps?

Treat them as evidence of execution, not as fields. Capture presence, date if legible, and the page. Do not ask the model to invent a signatory name from a flourish.

Can I add a seventh document type?

Yes. Copy a property set, list the required fields, pick a confidence floor from the closest neighbour, and version the schema. Table 3A is the worksheet for it.

What if the break is downstream of extraction?

Then it is a wiring problem, not an OCR problem. The free workflow diagnostic maps that path. This pack stays useful as the extraction layer underneath it.

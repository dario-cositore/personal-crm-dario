Part 1 · Speed-to-Lead Blueprint Pack

Seven stages, one pipeline: capture to assigned rep in under ten seconds, with the config and the failure behaviour written down.

Every form fill, ad conversion and chat message goes through the same seven stages: validation, dedupe, enrichment, scoring, routing and assignment. This pack is the buildable version of that pipeline. Stage by stage, with the keys you set and the way each stage fails.

The pipeline

One unified webhook endpoint accepts form fills, chat transcripts and ad conversions. Payloads get normalized and schema-checked before anything else happens. From there the lead moves left to right, then writes back and notifies.

ingress | v [01 Capture] -> [02 Validation] -> [03 Dedupe] -> [04 Enrich] -> [05 Scoring] | v [07 Assign] <- [06 Router] <- -----------------------------------+ | ^ | +---- conflict resolver (score or territory tie) v [CRM write] -> [Notifier: Slack / SMS / Email] -> [Dashboard readback] dead-letter queue + correlation log on every hop
StageNameJobTells you when it is wrong
01CaptureAccept one event shape from every sourceEvent count drops or payload shape changes
02ValidationReject garbage before it becomes a recordReject rate climbs above baseline
03DedupeMatch before create, merge with a paper trailNew record count spikes against stable traffic
04EnrichFirmographic and intent context in parallelProvider timeouts, cache hit rate falls
05ScoringOne fit and behaviour score, 0 to 100Score distribution shifts without a traffic change
06RouterTerritory, load and availability in one passUnresolved owners, territory conflicts queued
07AssignHand to a rep or queue, start the SLA clockNotification not acknowledged inside the window

Stage-by-stage build guide

01Capture gateway

Inputs: website and landing page forms, webinar and content downloads, Facebook Lead Ads, Google Ads conversions, LinkedIn Lead Gen Forms, Drift or Intercom chat transcripts.

Config: one public endpoint. Normalize every source into your internal event contract before anything downstream reads it. Preserve event_id, correlation_id, idempotency_key, source_system, source_record_id, utm_source, utm_campaign. Store the raw payload alongside the normalized one.

Guardrail: HMAC signature verification on the endpoint before processing. Unauthorized test submissions are a real source of junk records.

Failure behaviour: non-2xx to the provider, payload persisted for replay, alert if the error rate moves. Budget 150 ms.

02Validation

Inputs: the normalized event.

Config: schema and sanity checks. Required fields present. Email format valid. Consent captured where regulation needs it. Enum fields limited to allowed values. Budget and urgency checked against the list you actually route on. Session and UTM context passes through untouched.

Rule: reject to a review queue, do not write a partial record. A contact created with an empty required field is worse than no contact, because the follow-up sequence will fire on stale data.

Failure behaviour: reject count and reasons logged per field, dead-letter queue holds the payload. Budget 100 ms.

03Dedupe (Redis + fuzzy)

Inputs: validated event.

Config: deterministic match on email and phone. Fuzzy match on company name and normalized domain. Redis keyed on normalized values for the lookup, target under 100 ms. Merge strategy per field: first non-empty wins for contact fields, latest wins for behavioural fields, never overwrite a human correction.

Order of operations: search contact by email, search company by domain, search open deals by contact and pipeline, update when found, create only after the search clears. Store every object ID against the original event.

Failure behaviour: ties and near-matches go to the conflict resolver, never auto-merged. Every merge logged with its inputs. Budget 100 ms.

04Enrichment

Inputs: deduped identity (email, domain, company name).

Config: parallel lookups against providers such as Clearbit and Apollo, plus LinkedIn where your licence allows it. Cache keyed on domain with a TTL you can defend. Warm the cache on your top 200 domains before cutover.

Rule: a lead never waits on a lookup. Hard timeout per provider, then fall through to cached data and mark the record enrichment: partial.

Failure behaviour: circuit breaker per provider, staggered re-enable, alert when the cache hit rate falls below your floor. Budget 3000 ms including timeout.

05Scoring

Inputs: firmographic fit, behavioural interest, source, campaign, prior interaction history.

Config: one 0 to 100 score. Keep the inputs visible on the record: fit component, behaviour component, and the rules that fired. Retrain on conversion outcomes so routing decisions improve as the dataset grows.

Rule: if a model classifies messy text, force it to choose from a closed list and validate the choice: sales_priority, sales_standard, founder_review, nurture, reject_spam, manual_review_missing_data. Store the raw answer beside the summary. Raw pain tells you what the buyer said. Summaries compress, and compression loses edge.

Failure behaviour: score outside 0 to 100 or missing inputs routes to manual_review_missing_data. Budget 400 ms.

06Router

Inputs: score, territory, availability, existing owner, open deal status, customer status.

Config: territory rules by region, account book, segment or language. Load balancing across active reps. Account-based routing, round-robin and skill-based assignment run through the same engine so there is one place to inspect.

Rule: owner status is checked at routing time, not trusted from the record. An inactive owner ID is the fastest way to lose a lead. Log the decision: which rule, which evidence, which rep.

Failure behaviour: unresolved territory or tie goes to the conflict resolver, then to a manual queue with an SLA timer. The router keeps a dead-letter queue for anything it cannot place. Budget 250 ms.

07Assign and notify

Inputs: resolved owner, lead record, context note.

Config: write to CRM, then fire the notification hub: Slack, SMS, push and email with priority queuing. Include enough context in the alert that the rep does not open the CRM to decide whether to care.

SLA escalation: if the primary rep does not acknowledge inside the window, escalate to the next rep automatically, then to the team channel. Timer state lives with the lead so a restart does not lose it.

Failure behaviour: notification failure does not roll back the assignment, it pages. Budget 300 ms assign, 400 ms notify.

Weekly review checklist

Every dead-letter item from the week has an owner and a decision: replay, fix, or drop.
Duplicate merges reviewed, each one logged with its inputs.
Escalations counted, with the rule that fired each time.
Scoring model inputs still match the fields you actually capture.
Owner roster checked against active users in the CRM.
Latency budget reviewed per stage, not as a single end number.

Build order if you cannot do it all at once

  1. Capture and validation. One endpoint, one contract, rejects visible.
  2. Dedupe. Search before create beats every downstream cleanup.
  3. Assignment and notification. Even a crude router beats no router.
  4. Failure path. Dead-letter queue, correlation log, alerts that page.
  5. Enrichment and scoring. Add once the boring layers are trusted.
Why that order: the stages above the write path protect the record. Enrichment and scoring make the record smarter, they do not make it correct. Fix correctness first.

FAQ

Does this replace the systems blueprint?

No. The blueprint explains the architecture. This pack is the build version: keys, module order, payloads, budgets and thresholds.

Which orchestration tools does it assume?

Make.com for the outline, since it handles nested iterators and error handling well. Zapier, n8n and custom API receivers map to the same stage boundaries.

What if my volume is small right now?

Keep the same stage boundaries. Shrink the budgets, keep the dead-letter queue. Volume changes the sizing, not the shape.

Where does AI actually fit?

Classifying messy pain text, urgency and fit in scoring, with a closed list of allowed outputs and validation on the choice. The final routing path still gets a strict schema.

Before you start

Lead routing fails quietly. The form fires, the CRM creates a contact, the dashboard shows activity. Then the owner field is blank, the deal opened in the wrong pipeline, and the follow-up sequence never started because one routing field arrived as null. Nobody notices until the follow-up window has closed.

I wrote this layer for Meridian Legal Group, a firm running twelve intake channels across three offices. Their HubSpot instance had become a data graveyard: duplicate contacts in the thousands, leads assigned at random, and SLA timers that did not exist. The rebuild starts one layer below the alerts. It starts with the contract.

Ground rules: fix ownership before you touch speed. A lead owned in ten seconds by the wrong rep is still a lost handoff. Take the contract inventory before you change any workflow. Write down what the form actually sends while it is still true.

Section 2 · Priority order: existing relationship beats round robin

Most stacks have five routing rules fighting each other. Territory says one owner, service line says another, the campaign says another, round robin says another. Without a fixed priority order, routing becomes random with extra steps. This is the hierarchy I install. Read it top to bottom. The first rule that matches wins.

Rule merge checklist

One winner per lead: every test case resolves to exactly one owner, one pipeline, one stage.
Existing customer and open deal sit above every distribution rule: round robin never steals a live relationship.
Critical severity outranks campaign source: an urgent lead does not wait behind a nurture path.
Round robin pool contains active users only: a departed rep is not a fallback owner.
Every rule has a written reason string: the CRM will store it as routing_reason in Section 3.
Unmatched leads have a destination: the manual review queue is rule 9, not an accident.

Section 4 · Validate the owner, write to CRM, then alert

Order matters more than speed here. Deduplicate before assignment, never after. Validate the owner and the pipeline before the write. Send the alert only after the CRM confirms. An alert that fires before the write is a lie the sales team will believe.

STEP 1Store the raw event

What I check: the raw payload lands in storage before any transform runs.

Evidence to collect: the archived payload with event_id and received_at intact.

Pass condition: every source event is reconstructable even if the chain fails mid-flight.

Common failure: the webhook handler parses first and stores never. Then a vendor payload change destroys the evidence.

STEP 2Deduplicate before assignment

What I check: contact and company search runs before any owner is resolved.

Evidence to collect: match results by email, phone, company domain, and open-deal lookup.

Pass condition: one human, one contact record, one owner. Existing matches update instead of create.

Common failure: route first, dedupe later. Two reps receive the same buyer and the pipeline splits.

STEP 3Validate owner and pipeline state

What I check: the target owner exists, is active, holds the right team and territory, and can receive this source.

Evidence to collect: owner status response, pipeline existence, stage existence, open-deal check.

Pass condition: target_owner_id resolves to a live user and target_pipeline / target_stage both exist.

Common failure: a user left and the CRM kept the ID alive. Leads land in an inbox nobody reads.

STEP 4Write to CRM, then the routing reason

What I check: owner, pipeline, stage and the reason note all land in one confirmed write sequence.

Evidence to collect: API response codes, before and after values, the stored routing_reason.

Pass condition: the CRM is usable before anyone is notified. The record is reconstructable from its event_id.

Common failure: renamed property, expired token, or a retry that creates a second record instead of updating.

STEP 5Alert after the write confirms

What I check: the owner alert, the follow-up sequence and any SLA timer start only after CRM success.

Evidence to collect: alert timestamp versus CRM write timestamp, sequence enrollment id.

Pass condition: the rep receives a notification only when the CRM state is usable, with context in the message itself.

Common failure: Slack fires before HubSpot finishes. The rep opens a record that does not exist yet.

Idempotency key format

One key per source event. The key is what makes a replay safe: same key, same decision, no second assignment. Compute it at the edge and thread it through every write.

idempotency_key = source_system + ":" + source_record_id + ":" + normalized_email

Example:
lead_route:web_form:submission_8841:ops@example.com

Replay rule:
if idempotency_key already assigned -> return existing owner_id, write nothing
if idempotency_key in flight        -> return 409 to the provider, let it retry
if idempotency_key unknown          -> run the full path from Step 1

Replay and review checklist

Every failure lands somewhere visible: raw payload, decision attempt, error reason, named queue owner.
Replay is idempotent: re-running the same idempotency_key never creates a second owner assignment.
Max attempts are bounded: a stuck event moves to the dead-letter queue instead of retrying forever.
Rate limits slow the queue: CRM 429 responses back off instead of dropping payloads.
Manual review has an SLA: a human reads the queue on a schedule, not when someone remembers.
The routing reason is stored with the record: sales can read why the lead landed there without asking ops.
What Meridian proved: twelve intake channels across three offices, leads assigned within ten seconds of submission, and an audit trail that makes every assignment inspectable. The SLA timers that did not exist now escalate on their own. The rebuild was the routing layer, not the CRM edition.

Section 6 · FAQ

Do I need to rebuild the whole CRM to use this?

No. The kit is the routing layer: contract, hierarchy, decision object, validation gates, failure queue. It sits in front of whatever CRM you already run. The CRM stays. The ownership logic changes.

What if my form cannot send every contract field?

Then that source goes through the failure path for the fields it cannot fill, instead of guessing. Table 1B is where you record the gaps. A short diagnostic intake with extraction behind a strict schema is usually the cleaner fix than a longer form.

Where does dedupe belong in the chain?

Before assignment, always. Search contact by email and phone, company by domain, and look for open deals before any owner resolves. Dedupe after assignment creates two owners on one buyer and splits the pipeline.

Can Make, Zapier and n8n all execute this decision object?

Yes. That is the point of writing the decision once. The executor reads target_owner_id, target_pipeline, target_stage and routing_reason and performs the writes. Swap the executor without rewriting a single rule.

What happens when routing cannot resolve an owner?

The lead goes to the manual review queue with its raw payload, decision attempt and error reason. It never disappears. Unresolved is a state with an owner, not a silent stop.

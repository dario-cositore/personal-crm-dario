# Objection handling

## "It's too expensive"

- Do not discount. Ask: "Compared to what?" Usually they compare to doing nothing.
- Reframe: "The question is whether the hours you spend on this every week are worth more than the price."
- Offer a smaller scope at the same per-day rate, not a lower price.

## "Can you do it cheaper if we do part of it?"

- Yes, remove scope. Never reduce the rate.
- Write the reduced scope into the proposal.

## "We need to think about it"

- "Of course. What specifically do you want to think through? I can answer now or send a note."
- Set a follow-up date. Do not leave it open.

## "Our IT person can build this"

- "Probably. The question is whether they have the time and whether you want to maintain it. I hand over the keys either way."
- Offer the handover document as proof of transferability.

## "We already have a tool for that"

- "Which one? What does it not do that you need?"
- Position as the layer that connects the tools they already have.

## "AI makes mistakes"

- "Yes. That is why we test with your real cases before delivery, and why there is a human approval step on anything that goes out."
- Show the acceptance paragraph.

## "We don't have the data ready"

- "That is normal. Part of the sprint is collecting and structuring the first batch. We start with what you have."
